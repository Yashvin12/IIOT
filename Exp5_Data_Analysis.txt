THEORY:

Internet of Things (IoT) Data Analytics:
The IoT is a network of interconnected devices that collect
and share data automatically. In industrial settings (IIoT),
thousands of sensors continuously generate data such as
temperature, pressure, humidity, vibration, and flow rates.

IoT Data Analytics means collecting, examining and
interpreting this sensor data to:
  - Detect patterns and anomalies
  - Make data-driven decisions
  - Predict failures before they occur (Predictive Maintenance)
  - Optimize industrial processes

Four Main Components of IoT Data Analytics:

  1. Data Collection:
     IoT sensors (temperature, humidity, pressure, motion)
     collect raw data continuously from the environment.

  2. Data Storage:
     Massive sensor data is stored in cloud databases or
     local servers efficiently.

  3. Data Processing:
     Data cleaning, transformation, and normalization is
     done to prepare data for analysis. Removes errors and
     makes data consistent.

  4. Data Analysis and Visualization:
     Statistical tools, machine learning algorithms, charts
     and dashboards are used to find patterns and insights.

Why Excel for IoT Data Analysis:
  - Quick and accessible for small to medium datasets
  - Built-in chart and graph generation
  - Pivot Tables for data summarization
  - No programming required - ideal for beginners
  - Widely used in industry for quick data exploration

--------------------------------------------------------------
DATASET INFORMATION:

  Name    : Farm Temperature and Humidity
  Source  : Kaggle (kaggle.com/datasets)
  File    : Excel / CSV format (~86 KB)
  Period  : 3 months of readings
  Farms   : 4 farms, each 1 km apart
  Interval: Every 1 hour (24 readings per day)
  Total   : approx. 2160 readings per farm

  Column 1 : Temperature readings in Celsius (e.g. 28.5, 31.2)
  Column 2 : Humidity readings in % (e.g. 65.3, 72.1)
  Row 1    : Column headers
  Row 2+   : Hourly sensor data

--------------------------------------------------------------
PROCEDURE:

PART 1 - Download Dataset from Kaggle:
  1. Open browser > go to kaggle.com.
  2. Create a free account or sign in with Google.
  3. Search "Farm Temperature and Humidity" in search bar.
  4. Click the dataset > click Download button.
  5. Extract the ZIP file.
  6. Open the .csv or .xlsx file in Microsoft Excel.

PART 2 - Temperature Analysis (1 Day = 24 Hours):
  Goal: See how temperature changes during one full day.

  Steps:
  1. Open the Excel file.
  2. Look at Column A (Temperature readings).
  3. Select cells A2 to A25
     (Row 2 to Row 25 = 24 rows = 24 hours = 1 day).
  4. Click Insert tab in the top menu.
  5. Click Recommended Charts.
  6. Select Line Chart (best for time-series data).
  7. Click OK. The graph will appear.
  8. Observe the temperature pattern through the day.

  Expected Result (1 Day / 24 Hours):
  - Temperature is LOW at midnight (12 AM).
  - Rises gradually through the morning.
  - Reaches PEAK at 12 PM (noon) - maximum temperature.
  - Falls again in afternoon and evening.
  - Returns to LOW at night.

PART 3 - Temperature Analysis (2 Days = 48 Hours):
  Goal: Confirm the daily temperature cycle repeats.

  Steps:
  1. Select cells A2 to A49
     (48 rows = 48 hours = 2 full days).
  2. Click Insert > Recommended Charts > Line Chart > OK.
  3. Observe the pattern.

  Expected Result (2 Days / 48 Hours):
  - Peak at position 12 (12 PM Day 1).
  - Peak again at position 36 (12 PM Day 2) (24 + 12 = 36).
  - Minimum at position 24 (midnight between Day 1 and Day 2).
  - Confirms the CYCLIC daily temperature pattern.

PART 4 - Humidity Analysis (1 Day = 24 Hours):
  Goal: See how humidity changes during a day.

  Steps:
  1. Click on Column B (Humidity readings).
  2. Select cells B2 to B25 (24 hours of humidity data).
  3. Click Insert > Recommended Charts.
  4. Select Scatter Plot (recommended for humidity).
  5. Click OK and observe.

  Expected Result (1 Day Humidity):
  - Humidity is HIGHEST at night (inverse of temperature).
  - Humidity FALLS during the day as temperature rises.
  - Notable DIP around 1 PM (13th reading).

PART 5 - Humidity Analysis (2 Days = 48 Hours):
  Steps:
  1. Select cells B2 to B49 (48 hours).
  2. Insert > Scatter Plot.
  3. Observe pattern.

  Expected Result (2 Days Humidity):
  - Cyclic pattern: humidity drops at hour 13 (1 PM Day 1).
  - Drops again at hour 37 (24+13 = 1 PM Day 2).
  - Confirms temperature and humidity are INVERSELY related.

PART 6 - Repeat for Other Farms (Columns C, D, E, F):
  1. Select corresponding columns for farms 2, 3, 4.
  2. Repeat same charting procedure.
  3. Compare how temperature and humidity varies between farms.

--------------------------------------------------------------
CHART TYPES USED:

  Chart Type    Best Used For              When to Use
  ----------    -------------------        -------------------
  Line Chart    Continuous data over time  Temperature trends
  Scatter Plot  Discrete data, fluctuation Humidity variations
  Bar Chart     Comparing categories       Farm vs farm
  Histogram     Distribution of values    Frequency of ranges

--------------------------------------------------------------
USEFUL EXCEL FUNCTIONS:

  =AVERAGE(A2:A25)       : Average temperature for 1 day
  =MAX(A2:A25)           : Maximum temperature (peak)
  =MIN(A2:A25)           : Minimum temperature (night)
  =STDEV(A2:A25)         : Standard deviation (variability)
  =CORREL(A2:A25,B2:B25) : Correlation between temp & humidity
                           (should be negative value)

--------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO ADD CHART TITLE AND LABELS:
  1. Click on the chart.
  2. Click Chart Design > Add Chart Element > Chart Title
     > Above Chart.
  3. Type: "Temperature Variation - 24 Hours"
  4. Add Axis Titles:
     - X-axis: "Time (Hours)"
     - Y-axis: "Temperature (Celsius)"

--------------------------------------------------------------
RESULTS AND INFERENCES:

Temperature Results:
  1. 1-Day (24 hrs): Peak at hour 12 (noon), minimum at
     hours 0 and 24 (midnight).
  2. 2-Day (48 hrs): Peak at hour 12 and hour 36 - confirms
     the daily cyclic pattern.
  3. Farms near equator (India) show consistent daily swing.
  4. Small variation between 4 farms due to microclimate.

Humidity Results:
  1. 1-Day: Humidity peaks at night, dips at ~1 PM.
  2. 2-Day: Cyclic - humidity drops at hour 13 and hour 37.
  3. Inverse relationship with temperature confirmed.
  4. Scatter plots show more detail than line charts.
